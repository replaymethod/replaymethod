# Replay Method monetization strategy

## Decision

Replay Method should launch with a **useful, permanent free tier** and one paid
founding plan. The free result must be trustworthy enough to create an "aha"
moment. Payment should unlock frequency, memory and measurable continuity—not a
less honest diagnosis.

The launch ladder is:

| Plan | Price | Intended value |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Free | $0 | First complete replay analysis, then one focused check-in every 30 days; saved report; one active focus |
| Founding 100 | $9/month | Four complete analyses per month; cross-match recurrence; progress verification; evolving plan; priority processing |

The $9 price is sensible for a founding beta, but it is not cheap merely because
it is below $10. Trophi currently advertises a broad free plan and a discounted
$6/month annual Premium plan, while ReplayLabs offers one premium replay unlock
per week for free and paid plans from €3.99/month. Replay Method therefore earns
$9 only when the recurring improvement loop is visibly better than a one-off
report.

The first 100 paid founders should keep $9/month for their first 12 paid months
while continuously subscribed. This is valuable without creating an unlimited
lifetime liability. Joining the waitlist is not a purchase.

### Market conclusion

Free entry is common. Among these 22 examples, at least 18 visibly offer a free
product, free analysis, open beta, free core utility or meaningful free content.
The market is therefore not waiting for the word "free." The less common and more
interesting position is:

> a full-quality personal replay diagnosis for free, followed by a deliberately
> limited recurring check-in, with payment buying continuity and faster iteration.

That is narrower than a free stats dashboard and more defensible than a deliberately
weak "sample report."

## Why limited free can convert

### The useful psychology

1. **Risk removal.** A player cannot judge a personalized coach before it has seen
   their match. A real free result resolves that information gap.
2. **Value realization.** The first evidence-backed diagnosis changes the question
   from "is this real?" to "do I want this after my next matches?"
3. **Endowed progress.** A saved baseline, active focus and next measurement make
   the player feel that an improvement process has started.
4. **Loss of cadence, not loss of truth.** When the player wants another analysis
   before the monthly free reset, the frustration comes from waiting—not from
   discovering that the free report was fake.
5. **Accumulating memory.** Cross-match recurrence and progress history become more
   valuable over time, so the paid product feels like the natural continuation of
   the free product.

[Research on usage-limited freemium](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1567422322000370)
finds that a free tier needs to remain functional: too little free usage hurts
acquisition, while too much removes the incentive to upgrade. A [large two-year
randomized SaaS experiment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12217587/)
also found that deeper trial engagement can improve delayed conversion even when
it does not improve immediate conversion. That supports measuring 30-, 60- and
90-day conversion rather than judging the idea after the first session.

### The danger

Free can [anchor the reference price at zero](https://hbr.org/2025/06/the-risks-of-offering-free-goods-and-services),
attract support-heavy users and consume expensive compute. It also fails when paid
features are unrelated to the value users learned to love. Replay Method avoids
that by:

- naming the plan **Free**, not pretending that it is unlimited;
- showing the $9 founding plan beside the free plan from day one;
- keeping diagnosis quality the same across tiers;
- limiting report frequency and history, not correctness;
- making every paid benefit part of the same improvement loop;
- applying hard monthly cost and queue limits.

## Exact free-to-paid design

### Free

- first complete evidence-backed analysis;
- one focused check-in every rolling 30 days after account-based access launches;
- saved access to completed reports;
- one active coaching focus;
- same detector quality, confidence rules and abstention behavior as paid;
- no payment card required;
- clear next reset date.

The focused check-in may re-measure the active behavior on one supported replay. It
does not include unlimited new focuses, multi-match recurrence or historical trend
analysis. Until accounts and automated limits exist, the beta can safely begin with
only the first complete analysis.

### Founding 100

- four complete replay analyses per billing month;
- cross-match recurrence and trend detection;
- verification of whether the current focus is improving;
- adaptive next-focus selection;
- priority processing;
- one month of unused-analysis rollover, capped at four;
- $9/month for the first 12 paid months while continuously subscribed;
- cancel for future renewal at any time.

Four analyses supports a weekly improvement cadence and keeps early compute costs
predictable. "Unlimited" should not be sold before real usage and cost distributions
are known.

### Upgrade moments

Do not request a card before the first result. Show the paid offer:

1. after the player has viewed their evidence moments and active focus;
2. when they choose "track this across my next matches";
3. when they submit another replay before the free reset;
4. when a new match suggests that history is required to distinguish a pattern from
   a one-off event.

The primary message is not "unlock AI." It is:

> Keep the coach watching long enough to prove whether the fix worked.

## Pricing roadmap

Do not launch 3-, 6- and 12-month commitments during the founding beta. Every extra
plan increases choice, support and refund complexity before retention and costs are
known.

1. **Founding beta:** Free + $9 monthly only.
2. **After 8–12 weeks of paid cohort data:** decide whether the public monthly price
   should rise. A $12–$15 range is plausible only if recurring progress is real.
3. **After retention is proven:** add one annual option at roughly 20–25% below the
   public monthly total. Keep two paid choices, monthly and annual.
4. **Human review:** sell separately or in a higher tier; do not hide an unbounded
   human-service cost inside $9.

## Guarantee and refund design

Replay Method should not advertise a rank-up guarantee during beta. Rank depends on
matchmaking, teammates, play volume, player execution and variables the product does
not control. A vague promise with hidden escape clauses would damage trust, increase
chargebacks and risk misleading-marketing violations.

The launch promise is narrower and provable:

> Every supported report will show the evidence behind its primary finding, state
> confidence and give one measurable next action—or clearly say that the evidence
> is insufficient.

Later, after validated longitudinal data exists, an annual plan may test a transparent
**Progress Guarantee**. Eligibility should be visible in the product, not buried in
terms. A possible structure is:

- same verified game account and supported mode for the measurement period;
- minimum baseline and follow-up replay count;
- completion of the prescribed check-ins and practice task;
- no bans, account sharing, smurfing or manipulated files;
- claim within a clear window;
- one claim per member per 12 months;
- automatic eligibility tracker and an appeal route;
- remedy of two additional months free first, or a clearly defined refund if a true
  money-back guarantee is advertised.

This is anti-fraud through objective evidence, not through a deceptive "grey zone."
Consumer withdrawal rights and advertised guarantees must be reviewed for each sales
market before checkout goes live. In Sweden, online subscriptions are generally
covered by a [14-day withdrawal framework](https://www.konsumentverket.se/varor-och-tjanster/angerratt-abonnemang-tv-mobil-internet/),
and [marketing must be truthful and non-misleading](https://www.konsumentverket.se/for-foretag/marknadsforing/god-marknadsforingssed/).

## Unit economics guardrails

- Target at least 75–80% contribution margin after payment fees, taxes and variable
  processing costs.
- Keep total variable processing cost below 20–25% of net subscription revenue.
- Measure cost per successful report, not cost per upload attempt.
- Reject corrupt/unsupported files before expensive processing.
- Queue free reports separately and cap abuse by verified account, device/payment
  signals and rate limits without penalizing legitimate households.
- Never include routine human review in the $9 promise unless its frequency and cost
  are explicitly capped.

## What to measure before changing price

| Funnel stage | Primary measurement |
| --- | --- |
| Activation | Successful replay processed and report opened |
| Aha moment | Evidence moment viewed plus useful rating of 4/5 or 5/5 |
| Upgrade intent | Click on history, second analysis or progress verification |
| Conversion | Paid start after a successful free report |
| Retention | Paid members active at 30, 60 and 90 days |
| Product outcome | Active-focus metric improved on supported follow-up matches |
| Economics | Net revenue, processing cost and support time per active member |
| Trust | Refund rate, disputes, insufficient-evidence rate and false-positive reports |

Do not A/B-test several prices inside the first 100 unless traffic is high enough to
produce meaningful samples. First prove usefulness and retention at $9. Collect a
willingness-to-pay answer after the free report, but do not let that survey interrupt
the report itself.

## Build sequence

1. Ship one complete free analysis with a saved report and usefulness rating.
2. Add accounts, entitlements, usage counters and an exact free-reset timestamp.
3. Add cross-match history and active-focus verification.
4. Instrument the four upgrade moments.
5. Add checkout only when paid benefits actually exist.
6. Run the founding 100 cohort and review conversion, retention and unit economics.
7. Decide public monthly/annual pricing and any progress guarantee from measured data.
